# Mega Backdoor Roth: The Move That Depends Entirely on Your Plan Document

## Thesis

The Mega Backdoor Roth is real, powerful, and worth up to ~$46k of additional Roth space per year (2024 limits). It is also the single planning move where general advice is most useless, because whether you can do it depends entirely on three specific provisions in your employer's plan document — and most articles don't tell you which provisions to look for or how to find out.

**Reader:** High earners with employer 401k plans who've read about MBR but bounced off because their plan portal didn't make it obvious whether it was available. Anyone who has maxed regular 401k + backdoor Roth IRA and is looking for the next bucket.

## Beat 2 — The Working

### The Math

The 2024 §415(c) total contribution limit is **$69k** (under-50) / **$76.5k** (50+) — combined employee + employer + after-tax.

After your $23k employee deferral and your employer's match (say $10k), the remaining "after-tax bucket" is **~$36k**. That's the MBR space.

### The Three Required Plan Provisions

All three must be present for the strategy to work:

#### (1) After-Tax (Non-Roth) Contributions Allowed

Distinct from "Roth 401k contributions." Many plans don't offer this at all.

**Plan document language to look for:** "voluntary after-tax contributions" or "employee after-tax account"

#### (2) In-Plan Roth Conversion OR In-Service Distribution of After-Tax Sub-Account

Without one of these, your after-tax dollars sit there accruing pre-tax earnings until separation, which destroys most of the value.

#### (3) Frequent or Automatic Conversion Mechanism

- **Daily auto-conversion:** Ideal
- **Quarterly:** Good
- **Annual:** Workable
- **"By request only with paperwork":** Barely workable

### Plan Availability

Published estimates: ~40–45% of large employer plans support MBR, with significant variance by industry (tech: good; finance: medium; healthcare/government: often missing).

### How to Find Out for Your Specific Plan

1. **Plan SPD (Summary Plan Description)** — search for "after-tax" (not "Roth")
2. **Call the plan administrator** — ask about the three provisions specifically, NOT "do you offer mega backdoor Roth" (administrators often don't recognize the term)
3. **Pull the most recent Form 5500** — limited but sometimes informative

### Technical Traps

**The pro-rata rule trap:** If the after-tax account has accrued earnings, conversion is partially taxable on the earnings portion. Frequent auto-conversion eliminates this problem; infrequent conversion creates it.

**The match-cap trap:** Some plans count after-tax contributions against the same per-paycheck cap as employee deferrals, which can complicate maxing both.

### Worked Examples

**Tech employee, $250k salary, plan supports daily auto-conversion:**

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Regular 401k deferral | $23k |
| Employer match | $10k |
| After-tax → auto-converts to Roth daily | $36k |
| **Total annual Roth space from MBR** | **$36k** |

Over 20 years at 7% real: **~$1.5M of tax-free retirement assets** that wouldn't otherwise exist.

**Same person, no in-service distribution:**

After-tax dollars sit in the after-tax sub-account, earnings accrue pre-tax, conversion at separation creates a meaningful taxable event. Strategy still positive but materially less valuable.

## Key Talking Points

1. **Most articles explain the concept, not the eligibility check.** The eligibility check is the entire decision.
2. **"Does my plan allow MBR?" is the wrong question** to ask the administrator. The right question names the three specific provisions.
3. **The strategy without in-service distribution or in-plan conversion is a much weaker strategy.** People often don't notice that nuance.
4. **For people whose plans don't support MBR**, the Solo 401k path (if you have side income) is often more accessible than people realize.
5. **"You can't, your plan doesn't support it" is genuinely useful information** that saves people from chasing a phantom.

---

## Eligibility Checklist

Use this to determine if MBR is available to you:

- [ ] Does your plan allow voluntary after-tax (non-Roth) contributions?
- [ ] Does your plan allow in-plan Roth conversions OR in-service distributions of the after-tax sub-account?
- [ ] How frequently can conversions occur? (Daily/quarterly = good; annual/manual = weaker)
- [ ] What is your remaining §415(c) space after employee deferrals + employer match?

If any of the first two boxes are unchecked: MBR is not available in your current plan. Consider Solo 401k if you have self-employment income.
