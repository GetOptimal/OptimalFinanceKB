## Thesis

For 35 years, California's parent-child exclusion let children inherit Mom's house and keep her low Prop-13 assessment. Prop 19 (effective February 2021) substantially closed that door. Most planning content online is outdated — it describes strategies that worked pre-2021 and now either fail or actively trigger reassessment. The remaining live strategies are narrower than the popular framing suggests, and the timing windows are unforgiving.

**Reader:** California homeowners with appreciated property and adult children. Anyone planning around inheriting the family home. Anyone whose parents' "estate plan" was put in place before 2021.

---

## Beat 1 — The Claim

- "Put the kids on title before you die so they inherit the property tax base"
- "Add multiple children as co-tenants to dilute reassessment"
- "Use a trust to avoid reassessment at death"
- "California's parent-child exclusion preserves the assessment forever"

**Most of this advice was correct between 1986 and February 2021. Almost none of it works the same way now.**

---

## Beat 2 — The Working

### What Prop 19 Changed (Feb 16, 2021)

Three things at once:

1. **Parent-child exclusion now requires the child to use the property as their primary residence within 1 year**
2. **Even when the exclusion applies, only the first $1M of value increase is shielded** — anything above that gets partial reassessment
3. **The exclusion is gone entirely for non-primary-residence transfers** (vacation homes, rental properties)

### What Actually Still Works

| Strategy | Status | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Spousal transfers | ✅ Unchanged | Interspousal exclusion preserved; surviving spouse keeps assessment |
| Primary-residence parent-child transfer | ⚠️ Capped | $1M shield applies — useful but limited |
| Original Transferor / co-tenancy exclusion (R&T §62.3) | ⚠️ Narrow | Requires both co-tenants to occupy as primary residence for ≥1 year. Specific filing required within set deadlines |
| Legal entity ownership (LLC/partnership) | ⚠️ Complex | Preserves assessment until >50% change in ownership occurs. Introduces own costs |
| Pre-death transfers using $1M shield | ⚠️ Timing-dependent | Transfers before death have different treatment than those at death |

### What Used to Work and Now Doesn't

- **"Just put the kids on title"** — adding non-spouse co-tenants now triggers a proportional reassessment at the moment of addition for the percentage transferred
- **Inheriting a non-primary-residence rental** — full reassessment
- **The "Lebowski Loophole" trust strategies** that exploited timing — Prop 19 removed most of them

### Worked Example

**$3M Bay Area home, $400k assessed value, two adult children:**

| Scenario | New Assessment | Annual Property Tax |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Pre-Prop 19 (kids inherit) | $400k | ~$5k/year |
| Post-Prop 19, neither kid moves in | $3M | ~$36k/year |
| Post-Prop 19, one kid moves in (primary residence) | ~$1.4M ($1M shield) | ~$17k/year |

### Important Mechanics

- The co-tenancy exclusion requires both co-tenants to **actually occupy** as primary residence for the qualifying period — not a "add the kids to title" maneuver, more a "the kid genuinely lives there with you for years" arrangement
- Statutory deadlines for filing claims (BOE-58-AH for parent-child, BOE-58-H for grandparent-grandchild, etc.) are **unforgiving** — missing a 3-year window can cost the exclusion

## Beat 3 — Where I Might Be Missing Something

- **"Original Transferor" co-tenancy interpretation** has a small body of guidance and practitioners disagree on edge cases
- Some counties are interpreting the "primary residence within 1 year" requirement **more strictly** than others

## Key Talking Points

1. **The most common "California property tax planning" advice online is from before February 2021 and is now wrong, harmful, or both.**
2. **The co-tenancy exclusion exists, but requires actual co-occupancy** — it's not "put the kids on title last year."
3. **The $1M shield is real but capped** — for most appreciated coastal California property, it covers a fraction of the gain.
4. **LLC/trust structures still help in some cases**, but they're not a free lunch — they create their own ongoing costs and tax filings.
5. **The decision often has to be made before the parent's death**, sometimes years before. Waiting until probate is too late for most of the remaining strategies.
